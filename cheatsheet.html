Market equilibrium and surplus

Label: D = MB = WTP, S = MC, P1, Q1, CS, PS; Qs = amount sellers offer, Qd = amount buyers want at a given price

  1. Draw D and S; mark where they cross: P1, Q1.
  2. Shade CS (under D, above P1) and PS (above S, below P1).
  • CS = ½ × Q1 × (where D hits the P axis − P1).
  • PS = ½ × Q1 × (P1 − where S hits the P axis).
  • Total surplus CS + PS is largest at Q1, so there is no DWL.
  • Above P1: Qs > Qd (surplus), P falls. Below: shortage, P rises.

Shifting demand and supply

Label: D1 to D2 (or S1 to S2) with an arrow; P1, Q1, P2, Q2

  1. Buyer changes (income, tastes) shift D; cost changes shift S.
  2. Draw the shift with an arrow; mark the new crossing P2, Q2.
  • D right (more demand): P up, Q up. D left: P down, Q down.
  • S right (more supply): P down, Q up. S left: P up, Q down.
  • Both shift: D right and S left give P up; both right give Q up; the other one depends on which shift is bigger.
  • The good's own price moves along a curve; it never shifts it.

Elasticity: steep versus flat demand

Label: steep D = inelastic, flat D = elastic; S1 to S2; P1, Q1, P2, Q2

  1. Draw the same S1 to S2 shift on both graphs.
  2. Steep D: P changes a lot, Q a little. Flat D: the reverse.
  • ε (elasticity) = % change in Q ÷ % change in P: how strongly buyers react. Negative for D; |ε| = its size.
  • On a straight D: ε = (1 ÷ slope of D) × (P ÷ Q) at that point.
  • |ε| > 1 elastic, |ε| < 1 inelastic. Vertical D: 0. Flat D: infinite.
  • Spending P × Q rises with P if inelastic, falls if elastic.

Price ceiling (and floor)

Label: Pceil = legal maximum price; at Pceil, Qs = amount sellers offer (on S), Qd = amount buyers want (on D)

  1. Draw D and S crossing at P1, Q1. Neither curve shifts.
  2. Flat Pceil line below P1. Shade DWL from Qs to Q1.
  • Matters only below P1. Units sold = Qs. Shortage = Qd − Qs.
  • DWL = ½ × (Q1 − Qs) × (height of D at Qs − Pceil).
  • PS falls. CS can rise (lower price) or fall (fewer units).
  • Floor (legal minimum) above P1: sold = Qd, surplus = Qs − Qd.

Per-unit tax

Label: D = MB, S = MC, S + t, P1, Q1, Q2, Pbuyers, Psellers; t = tax per unit, Pbuyers = price buyers pay, Psellers = price sellers keep

  1. Draw D and S at P1, Q1, then S + t: S moved up by t.
  2. Q2: S + t meets D. Read Pbuyers on D; Psellers = Pbuyers − t on S.
  3. Shade revenue box (0 to Q2), DWL from Q2 to Q1, CS, PS.
  • Tax revenue = t × Q2. DWL = ½ × t × (Q1 − Q2).
  • Steeper side pays more. Same if buyers pay legally (D − t).
  • Burden per unit: buyers Pbuyers − P1, sellers P1 − Psellers.

Subsidy

Label: D = MB, S = MC, S − s, P1, Q1, Q2, Psellers, Pbuyers; s = subsidy per unit, Psellers = price sellers get, Pbuyers = price buyers pay

  1. Draw D and S at P1, Q1, then S − s: S moved down by s.
  2. Q2: S − s meets D (Q2 > Q1). Pbuyers on D; Psellers = Pbuyers + s.
  3. Shade the cost box (0 to Q2) and DWL from Q1 to Q2.
  • Government cost = s × Q2. DWL = ½ × s × (Q2 − Q1).
  • DWL because units from Q1 to Q2 have MC > MB.
  • CS gain + PS gain = government cost − DWL.

PPC (production possibilities curve)

Label: axes C (consumption), I (investment); PPC1; points a (on), d (inside), c (outside); PPC2 = growth

  1. Bowed-out PPC1 joining the two intercepts; mark a, d, c.
  2. Growth (more workers, machines, tech): PPC2 farther out.
  • Slope = −OC of one more C. Bowed out: that OC rises.
  • Inside = unemployment or recession, not an inward shift. More I today = PPC farther out later.

Comparative advantage: joint PPC

Label: intercepts, kink (where slope changes), slopes = OCs, gain

  1. Intercepts = sum of everyone's max: 60 Y, 30 X in 10 hours.
  2. From 60 Y, the person with lower OC of X goes first (Ann, slope −1) to the kink, then Ben (slope −4) to 30 X.
  • OC of X = Y per hour ÷ X per hour (Ann 1, Ben 4). Lower OC of X = comparative advantage: make X.
  • Three or more people: add pieces by OC of X, lowest first.

Budget line and spending rule

Label: food up, clothing across; I = income; Pf, Pc = prices; qf, qc = amounts bought; intercepts I/Pf, I/Pc; slope −Pc/Pf

  1. Join I ÷ Pf on the food axis and I ÷ Pc on clothing.
  2. Income up (I'): parallel out. Pc up (Pc'): pivot in on clothing.
  • Rational Spending Rule: MUa ÷ Pa = MUb ÷ Pb (MU = extra satisfaction from one more unit; a, b = any two goods).
  • Pc up: MUc ÷ Pc falls, so buy less clothing until equal again.

Competitive firm and market

Label: market: D, S = sum of firms' MC, P1, Q1; firm: flat d = MR = P at P1 (d = demand facing one firm), MC = firm supply, q*, PS

  1. Market: D and S cross at P1. Carry P1 across: flat d = MR = P.
  2. Firm: rising MC; best output q* where MC = P; shade PS.
  • MR (marginal revenue) = extra revenue from one more unit; a firm too small to move the price gets MR = P.
  • Produce where P = MC, so firm supply = its MC curve.
  • Industry S: add all firms' q (one firm's quantity) at each P.
  • Profit = PS − fixed cost. Firms enter if PS > fixed cost.

Monopoly

Label: D = MB; MR = marginal revenue; MC; Qm, Pm = monopoly Q and P; Qc, Pc = competitive Q and P; CS, transfer, PS, DWL

  1. D, then MR: same P intercept, twice as steep; rising MC.
  2. Qm where MR = MC; go straight up to D for Pm.
  3. Qc, Pc where D meets MC. DWL from Qm to Qc.
  • D: P = a − bQ (a = intercept, b = slope) gives MR = a − 2bQ.
  • Transfer: CS in the box between Pc and Pm becomes profit.
  • Profit = transfer + PS = revenue − area under MC.
  • Given Pm, Qm, flat MC: a = 2 × Pm − MC, b = (Pm − MC) ÷ Qm.

Negative externality and Pigouvian tax

Label: PMC = sellers' cost, EMC = harm to others, SMC = PMC + EMC; D = PMB = SMB (private, social benefit); Q1, Q*, DWL, tax

  1. D and S = PMC cross at Q1. Draw SMC, EMC above S.
  2. Q* (best amount, usually not 0) and P* where SMC meets D. DWL from Q* to Q1, pointing at Q*.
  • DWL = ½ × EMC × (Q1 − Q*) if EMC is constant.
  • Pigouvian tax = EMC at Q* (per unit) moves the market to Q*.
  • Positive externality: SMB = PMB + EMB (benefit to others) above D; too little made; subsidy = EMB at Q*.

Elasticity

  • Terms: ε = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP. %Δ = (new − old) ÷ old. |ε| = ε without its minus sign. Elastic: |ε| > 1. Inelastic: |ε| < 1. Unit elastic: |ε| = 1. Spending = P × Q.
If the question asksDo this

Surplus, efficiency, DWL

  • Terms: WTP = most a buyer would pay (D = MB = WTP; S = MC). CS = WTP − P, PS = P − MC, summed over units. Efficient = most CS + PS. DWL (deadweight loss) = surplus lost versus the efficient Q.
If the question asksDo this
Find CS, PSSolve D = S. CS = ½ × Q1 × (D intercept − P1). PS = ½ × Q1 × (P1 − S intercept).
Why is it CS (PS)?Buyers value units at WTP, pay P. Area under MC = variable cost, so PS = revenue − it.
Is it efficient?Yes if MB = MC, highest-MB buyers buy, lowest-MC sellers sell. Not always fair.
Shade the DWLTriangle from actual to efficient Q (too much or too little): ½ × Q gap × (MB − MC).
  • Trap Mistake: Q in thousands, area 25 = $25. Correct: $25,000.

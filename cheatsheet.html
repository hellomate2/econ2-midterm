Market equilibrium and surplus

Label: D = MB = WTP; S = MC; P1, Q1; CS; PS; surplus, shortage

  1. Draw D and S; mark the crossing P1, Q1.
  2. Shade CS under D above P1, and PS above S below P1.
  • CS = ½ × Q1 × (D intercept − P1). PS = ½ × Q1 × (P1 − S intercept).
  • Total surplus = CS + PS is largest at Q1 (no DWL).
  • Above P1: surplus, P falls. Below P1: shortage, P rises.

Shifting demand and supply

Label: D1 to D2 or S1 to S2 with an arrow; P1, Q1 and P2, Q2

  1. Buyer side (income, tastes) moves D; cost side moves S.
  2. Shift it with an arrow; mark the new crossing P2, Q2.
  • D right: P up, Q up. D left: P down, Q down.
  • S right: P down, Q up. S left: P up, Q down.
  • D right, S left: P up, Q unclear. Both right: Q up, P unclear.
  • The good's own price moves along a curve, never shifts it.

Elasticity: steep versus flat demand

Label: steep = inelastic; flat = elastic; S1, S2; P1, Q1, P2, Q2

  1. Draw the same S1 and S2 on both graphs.
  2. Steep D: P moves a lot, Q a little. Flat D: the reverse.
  • |ε| = |%ΔQ ÷ %ΔP| = |1 / slope| × (P / Q). |ε| > 1 elastic.
  • |ε| < 1 inelastic. Vertical D: ε = 0. Horizontal D: infinite.
  • Spending P × Q rises with P if inelastic, falls if elastic.

Price ceiling (and floor)

Label: D = MB = WTP, S = MC, P1, Q1, ceiling, Qs, Qd, shortage

  1. Draw D and S crossing at P1, Q1. Neither curve shifts.
  2. Flat ceiling below P1. Read Qs on S and Qd on D there.
  3. Shade DWL from Qs to Q1. CS and PS stop at Qs.
  • Binds only below P1. Traded Q = Qs. Shortage = Qd − Qs.
  • DWL = ½ × (Q1 − Qs) × (height of D at Qs − ceiling).
  • CS may rise (lower price) or fall (fewer units). PS falls.
  • Floor binds only above P1: surplus = Qs − Qd, traded Q = Qd.

Per-unit tax

Label: D = MB, S = MC, S + t, P1, Q1, Q2, wedge t, Pbuyers, Psellers

  1. Draw D and S at P1, Q1, then S + t: S moved up by t.
  2. Q2 is where S + t meets D (wedge t between D and S).
  3. At Q2 read Pbuyers on D, then Psellers = Pbuyers − t on S.
  4. Shade revenue box (0 to Q2), DWL from Q2 to Q1, CS, PS.
  • Revenue = t × Q2. DWL = ½ × t × (Q1 − Q2).
  • Same result if buyers legally pay (draw D − t).
  • Steeper side bears more: buyers Pbuyers − P1, sellers P1 − Psellers.

Subsidy

Label: D = MB, S = MC, S − s, P1, Q1, Q2, wedge s, Psellers, Pbuyers

  1. Draw D and S at P1, Q1, then S − s: S moved down by s.
  2. Q2 is where S − s meets D, so Q2 > Q1.
  3. At Q2 read Pbuyers on D, then Psellers = Pbuyers + s on S.
  4. Shade cost box (0 to Q2) and DWL triangle from Q1 to Q2.
  • Cost = s × Q2. DWL = ½ × s × (Q2 − Q1).
  • DWL because units from Q1 to Q2 have MC > MB.
  • CS gain + PS gain = cost − DWL.

PPC (production possibilities curve)

Label: axes C and I; PPC1 bowed out; points a (on), d (inside), c (outside); PPC2 = growth

  1. Bowed-out PPC1 between the two intercepts; mark a, d, c.
  2. Growth (more labor, capital, technology): PPC2 outside.
  • Slope = −OC of the horizontal good. Bowed out = rising OC.
  • Inside = recession, not an inward shift. More I today = bigger PPC later.

Comparative advantage: joint PPC

Label: intercepts; dashed line; kink; slopes = OCs; gain area

  1. Intercepts = sum of maxes (60 Y, 30 X); join with dashes.
  2. From 60 Y, slope −1 (Ann, lower OC of X) to the kink (20, 40), then slope −4 (Ben) to 30 X.
  • OC of X = Y per hour ÷ X per hour (Ann 1, Ben 4). Ann, lower OC, makes X.
  • Three or more people: add them in order of OC of X, lowest first; equal OCs share one segment.

Budget line and spending rule

Label: food vertical, clothing horizontal; intercepts I/Pf, I/Pc (I = income); slope −Pc/Pf

  1. Join I ÷ Pf (food axis) and I ÷ Pc (clothing axis).
  2. Income up: parallel out. Pc up: pivot in on clothing.
  • Rational Spending Rule: MUa ÷ Pa = MUb ÷ Pb.
  • Pc up: MUc ÷ Pc falls below MUf ÷ Pf, so buy less clothing, maybe more food, until equal.

Competitive firm and market

Label: market: D, S = sum of firm MC, P1, Q1; firm: flat d = MR = P at P1, MC = firm supply, q*, PS

  1. Market: D and S cross at P1. Carry P1 across: flat d = MR = P.
  2. Firm: rising MC; q* where MC crosses P; shade PS.
  • MR = P, so produce where P = MC. Firm supply = MC curve.
  • Industry S = horizontal sum: add all firms' q at each P.
  • Enter if PS area > fixed cost. Profit = PS − fixed cost.

Monopoly

Label: D = MB; MR (same intercept, twice as steep, hits Q axis halfway); MC; Qm, Pm, Qc, Pc; CS, transfer, PS, DWL

  1. Draw D, MR from the same intercept, rising MC.
  2. Qm where MR = MC; go straight up to D for Pm.
  3. Qc, Pc (competitive): D meets MC. DWL Qm to Qc.
  • P = a − bQ gives MR = a − 2bQ. No supply curve.
  • Profit = transfer + PS = revenue − area under MC.
  • Transfer: the CS rectangle between Pc and Pm becomes profit.
  • Given Qm and Pm: demand intercept a = 2 × Pm − MC, slope b = (Pm − MC) ÷ Qm.

Negative externality and Pigouvian tax

Label: D = PMB = SMB; S = PMC; SMC = PMC + EMC, EMC gap; Q1 at D = PMC; Q* at D = SMC; DWL; tax

  1. D and S = PMC cross at Q1. SMC sits EMC above S.
  2. Q* where SMC meets D. DWL from Q* to Q1 points at Q*.
  • Pollution above 0 is optimal: early units have SMB > SMC.
  • DWL = ½ × EMC × (Q1 − Q*) if EMC is constant.
  • Pigouvian tax = EMC at Q* moves the market to Q*.
  • Positive: SMB = PMB + EMB above D; too little made; subsidy = EMB at Q*.

Opportunity cost and the PPC

  • OC = the value of the next best alternative you give up.
  • Time: hours times your wage. Owned item: resale value now, not what you paid. Sunk costs: ignore.
  • College = tuition, fees, books + foregone earnings. Rent and food cancel (paid either way).
  • $200 ticket plus 3 hours at a $36 wage: OC = $308.
  • PPC slope = minus the OC of the horizontal-axis good.

Comparative advantage

  • OC of 1 X = (Y per hour) / (X per hour), for each person.
  • Lower OC = comparative advantage: specialize in it. Absolute advantage (more per hour) does not decide.
  • Same % gain in one good for everyone, or in both goods for one person: OCs keep their order, so nothing changes.
  • Equal OCs: no gains from specializing.
  • Trade price lies between the two OCs, so both gain.
  • "Always" or "solely" claims are usually false. Reasons not to specialize fully: national security, infant industries, different environmental or labor laws.

  • The reverse of any row shifts that curve left.
  • Own price change = movement along the curve (Qd or Qs). Anything else shifts it.
  • Buyer news: D. Seller news: S. Price control: neither.
  • Both shift: D right + S left: P up, Q depends on the bigger shift. Both right: Q up, P depends.
  • P and Q move together: D shifted. Opposite ways: S.
  • One curve shifts: the steeper the other curve, the more P moves and the less Q moves. Other curve vertical (seats, planted crops): only P moves. Horizontal: only Q moves.
  • Market D = horizontal sum of buyers' Qd at each P (it kinks where a buyer drops out). Labor market: firms demand, workers supply, wage = P.

Households

  • MU = change in total utility from one more unit. Total utility peaks where MU reaches 0.
  • Rational Spending Rule: MUa ÷ Pa = MUb ÷ Pb.
  • MU falls with each extra unit, so demand slopes down.
  • Pa rises: buy less a (MUa rises), maybe more b (MUb falls), until equal.
  • A change in MU at every Q (heatwave, taste) shifts demand. Own price change: move along it.
  • Log utility: MU = 1 ÷ c. Two people's combined utility peaks when their MUs (so c) are equal: moving $1 from richer to poorer raises it.

Elasticity

  • ε = %ΔQ ÷ %ΔP, with %Δ = (new − old) ÷ old.
  • ε = (1 ÷ slope) × (P ÷ Q), where slope = ΔP ÷ ΔQ.
  • Demand ε is negative; judge size: −0.5 is inelastic (0.5 < 1).
  • Spending: %Δ(P × Q) ≈ %ΔP + %ΔQ for small changes; for big ones compute P × Q before and after.

  • Reverse use: %ΔP = %ΔQ ÷ ε.
  • Inelastic: no close substitute, a necessity, the short run. Elastic: close substitute, easy to do without, the long run, a narrow good (one brand).
  • Supply: inelastic in the short run or with a fixed resource (oil wells, planted crops); elastic in the long run.
  • A straight demand line is unit elastic at its midpoint: elastic above it, inelastic below.
  • Inelastic = steep, elastic = flat.

Surplus, efficiency, DWL

  • CS = area under D (willingness to pay) and above the price.
  • PS = area above S (MC) and below the price.
  • Efficient = most total surplus: produce to MB = MC, highest-MB buyers buy, lowest-MC sellers sell.
  • DWL triangle points toward the efficient Q and grows out to the actual Q. Too little or too much Q both cause it.
  • DWL = ½ × (Q gap) × (MB − MC gap at the actual Q).
  • First Welfare Theorem: competitive equilibrium with no market failure maximizes total surplus. It ignores equity: willingness to pay depends on income.

Price controls

  • A ceiling binds only below P1, a floor only above. No curve shifts.
  • Q traded = the smaller of Qs and Qd at the controlled P.
  • One seller pricing below market with a purchase limit acts like a ceiling on its shelves: shortage, rationed by the limit, not by WTP.
  • Total surplus falls. CS can rise or fall: it gains a rectangle from PS, loses a triangle.
  • Ceiling misallocation: units may not reach the highest-MB buyers, a loss on top of the DWL.
  • Model misses fairness (gouging feels unfair) and market power.

Taxes and subsidies

  • A tax t is a vertical wedge t between D and S at Q2: buyers pay Pbuyers on D, sellers keep Pbuyers − t on S.
  • Statutory incidence (who pays the government) does not matter: a tax on sellers shifts S up by t, on buyers D down by t. Economic incidence = who bears it.
  • The more inelastic (steeper) side bears more.
Flat S or vertical Dbuyers pay all
Vertical S or flat Dsellers pay all
  • Buyers' price rises by less than t unless S is flat or D vertical.
  • DWL is bigger when D or S is elastic, zero if either is vertical.
  • A tax in one small area: sellers can sell elsewhere, so supply is flat there and buyers bear it.
  • No misallocation: price still rations.
  • Real pass-through can be uneven (a Finnish sales tax cut was about half passed on, a rise nearly all). The model is a guide, not gospel.
  • Price against tax across places: slope 1 = buyers bear all; slope 0 = sellers bear all; between = shared; above 1 = something else differs too (richer places have higher costs and taxes).

Competitive firms

  • Profit = revenue − total cost (counting the OC of every input).
  • Price taker: too small to move P, so it faces flat demand and MR = P.
  • Produce where MR = MC, so P = MC. Firm supply = its MC curve.
  • MC counts only costs that rise with one more unit (materials, hourly labor). Land, buildings, rent are fixed: they change profit, not MC or Q, and block entry in the short run, much less over years.
  • Trap Mistake: profit = PS. Correct: profit = PS − fixed cost. Enter only if PS (area between P and MC) > fixed cost.
  • Input price rises: every MC up, S left (most for sellers who use the input most). Better technology: MC down, S right. Entry: S right.
  • Free entry: profit draws entrants (S right, P falls) until profit = 0. Losses cause exit.

Monopoly

  • Market power: few close substitutes. Barriers to entry: high fixed costs, patents, licenses.
  • MR < P: to sell one more unit it must cut P on every unit, losing revenue on units already sold.
  • 1. Qm where MR = MC. 2. Go up from Qm to demand and read Pm.
  • Trap Mistake: P where MR meets MC. Correct: read P from demand, not from MC.
  • Constant MC = c: Qm = (a − c) ÷ (2b), Pm = (a + c) ÷ 2, competitive Q = 2 × Qm.
  • Patents: invention has a big fixed cost and low MC, so copies would push P to MC. A temporary monopoly pays for invention, with DWL until it expires.
  • Policy: antitrust law (targets dominance and practices like paying to be the default, not only prices), price regulation, government ownership, patent limits.

Externalities

  • Externality: a cost or benefit that falls on people outside the market and is left out of the price.
  • SMC = PMC + EMC, SMB = PMB + EMB. Market Q1: D = S. Efficient Q*: SMB = SMC.

  • External cost = area between SMC and PMC out to Q. Total surplus = CS + PS − external cost (+ tax revenue).
  • Monopoly plus pollution: compare Qm (MR = PMC) with Q* (D = SMC). The monopoly cut can offset overproduction; landing exactly on Q* is luck.

  • Coase: clear property rights plus cheap bargaining let the parties fix it privately.
  • Cap and trade: cap permits at Q*, firms trade them (permit price = EMC at Q*), so the cheapest cuts happen first.
